# A: Kobold Lair

A band of kobolds recently stole a dragon egg and brought it to these caves to eat. But when the egg hatched and a screeching dragon wyrmling emerged, the kobolds quickly realized they bit off more than they could chew.

The kobolds tried to appease the fussy wyrmling with treasure, but they can't agree on what to do next. Should they return the wyrmling to its nest, or should they raise it themselves?

Summary. The beginning of each cave presents its central conflict, as well as optional ways to increase or decrease the difficulty. If the characters are level 1, don't increase the difficulty.

Bold Terms. When a monster's name appears in **Bold** type, that's a visual cue to say, "Hey, DM, you should get this stat block ready. You're going to need it."

You can add monsters, such as [Giant Fire Beetles](bestiary.html#giant%20fire%20beetle_xmm), to the cave to make this scenario longer and more difficult, or you can reduce the number of [Kobold Warriors](bestiary.html#kobold%20warrior_xmm) to make it easier and shorter.

## Getting Started

To begin, read the following boxed text aloud:

A tunnel made of hard-packed earth leads into darkness. The cave echoes with high-pitched bickering overtopped by a loud, bestial squeal.

Boxed Text. Text that appears in a box like the one in this section is meant to be read aloud or paraphrased for the players when their characters first arrive at a location or under a specific circumstance, as described in the text.

The sounds come from the quarreling kobolds in [area A4](#hotb,1,a4%3a%20common%20area,0) and the crying wyrmling in [area A5](#hotb,1,a5%3a%20larder,0).

## Kobold Lair Features

The cave has the following features:

* Ceilings. The cave's ceilings are 10 feet high.
* Environment. The cave has rough stone walls and a dirt floor. The floor is littered with gnawed animal bones and junk collected by the kobolds.
* Lighting. The cave is unlit, and its denizens rely on Darkvision or other means to see. Area descriptions assume the characters have a light source or other means of seeing in [Darkness](variantrules.html#darkness_xphb).

Features. This section presents special features about the location. Some of these exist to help you set the mood—you might describe the crunch of animal bones under the characters' feet—while others detail important rules, such as secret doors and how to find them.

Exploration and Movement. Exploration is open-ended; the players don't take turns moving around the dungeon the way they do in combat. As they explore, direct the players to freely move around on the map, but stop them when they reach the entrance (area A1).

### A1: Entrance

A twenty-foot-long tunnel leads into the cave.

This tunnel contains a hidden pit trap, as detailed below.

Pit Trap. This 10-foot-deep pit is covered by a large, dirty cloth. When a character steps on the cloth, it gives way, dumping the character into the pit, at which point the pit is no longer hidden. A creature that falls into the pit takes 1d6 Bludgeoning damage.

Rules for detecting and escaping the trap are as follows:

* Detecting the Trap. The pit is poorly camouflaged. A character who examines the trapped area easily discovers the pit. Once detected, the pit can be safely avoided by sidling along its perimeter.
* Escaping the Pit. The pit's walls have cracks big enough to serve as handholds, allowing trapped creatures to climb out easily.

A character falling into the pit alerts the kobold in area A2, who approaches the pit and crows, "Got 'em!" The encounter in that area then occurs here.

Rhythm of Play. Here's an example of how the pit trap in area A1 might play out:

* **Describe the Scene**. Read the boxed text aloud.
* **Ask the Players, "What Do You Do?"** You might ask how the player characters enter the cave. If they charge brashly ahead, the character in the lead falls into the pit. However, if a player says their character looks carefully, they spot the trap.
* **Narrate the Results**. Once the pit's presence becomes known, describe the pit and the narrow ledge around it.

Once the players know the pit is there, ask them how their characters bypass it. If they come up with a creative solution not described in the text, err on the side of leniency and fun. Whenever you think a rule might be necessary, see the [D&D Beyond Basic Rules](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024).

Repeat this process for the other areas in this cave, modifying it based on their contents.

### A2: Guard Nook

[![](img/adventure/HotB/006-01-004.kobold-warrior-c.webp)](img/adventure/HotB/006-01-004.kobold-warrior-c.webp "Art credit: Sam Keiser")

Sam Keiser

A small, bipedal reptile stands guard in this nook.

A [Kobold Warrior](bestiary.html#kobold%20warrior_xmm) keeps watch in this nook. The guard is initially [Indifferent](variantrules.html#indifferent%20%5battitude%5d_xphb) toward intruders, but it's also naive. Rather than alerting the other kobolds when it spots the characters, it marches toward them and asks what they want.

Attitudes. Every creature has a starting attitude toward the characters: [Friendly](variantrules.html#friendly%20%5battitude%5d_xphb), [Hostile](variantrules.html#hostile%20%5battitude%5d_xphb), or [Indifferent](variantrules.html#indifferent%20%5battitude%5d_xphb) (see the [D&D Beyond Basic Rules](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024)). Regardless, creatures defend themselves in combat.

As an [Influence](actions.html#influence_xphb) action, a character can try to appease the kobold and make one of the following ability checks. Consider these examples:

**DC 10 Charisma (Deception).** The character tricks the kobold with an outlandish lie.

**DC 10 Charisma (Performance).** The character amuses the kobold with a jaunty tune.

**DC 10 Charisma (Persuasion).** The character convinces the kobold the party means it no harm.

On a successful check, the character improves the kobold's attitude to [Friendly](variantrules.html#friendly%20%5battitude%5d_xphb), and the kobold grants them entry, warning them cryptically about the "many-legs" to the west (see area A3). On a failed check, the kobold becomes [Hostile](variantrules.html#hostile%20%5battitude%5d_xphb) and flees toward [area A4](#hotb,1,a4%3a%20common%20area,0), yelping a call to arms.

Ability Checks. When the outcome of an action is uncertain, the game uses a d20 roll to determine success or failure. This is called a [D20 Test](variantrules.html#d20%20test_xphb). An ability check is one type of D20 Test. Here's how to run the ability check in area A2:

* **Describe the Scene**. Paint a mental picture of the kobold either by describing its words and actions or by actively roleplaying it—giving it a voice like an actor in a role.
* **Ask the Players, "What Do You Do?"** If the players aren't sure what to do, suggest ways they could respond to the kobold, like trying to deceive or persuade it.
* **Narrate the Results**. The Difficulty Class (DC) for each listed ability check is 10. If the total of the d20 and its modifiers equals or exceeds 10, the check succeeds. On a 9 or lower, it fails. In either case, the area's description tells you what happens.

* Example. A Rogue tries to deceive the kobold with a Charisma (Deception) check. The player rolls a d20 and adds their character's Deception modifier (+4) for a total of 14. The check succeeds—the kobold believes the lie!

### A3: Centipedes' Den

This cave contains heaps of waste. Two giant centipedes skitter about, picking at the refuse.

Two [Giant Centipedes](bestiary.html#giant%20centipede_xmm) scavenge this cave. The centipedes are [Hostile](variantrules.html#hostile%20%5battitude%5d_xphb) toward all other creatures and fight anyone who enters their den.

The kobolds are afraid of the centipedes—a fact they report to any who mention the beasts. If the characters slay the centipedes and show proof to a kobold, this deed earns the kobold's respect, improving its attitude toward them to [Friendly](variantrules.html#friendly%20%5battitude%5d_xphb).

Combat. Though the characters can avoid combat elsewhere, the centipedes in area A3 fight them on sight. Here's how that might play out:

* **Describe the Scene**. Read the boxed text aloud. Then say, "Roll Initiative!" to signal the start of combat. Roll once for both centipedes, which act on the same turn.
* **Players React**. In combat, the characters and the centipedes act on their turns. Use the "[Combat](book.html#xphb,1,combat)" section in the [D&D Beyond Basic Rules](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024) to run this encounter.

### A4: Common Area

This common area contains straw beds and a pile of junk. Five small, bipedal reptiles pace about the cave, arguing loudly.

Five [Kobold Warriors](bestiary.html#kobold%20warrior_xmm) are trying to decide whether to keep the wyrmling or return it to its nest. They cease bickering when they notice the characters. The kobolds' initial attitude is [Indifferent](variantrules.html#indifferent%20%5battitude%5d_xphb). If they learn the characters eliminated the guard in [area A2](#hotb,1,a2%3a%20guard%20nook,0), these kobolds are [Hostile](variantrules.html#hostile%20%5battitude%5d_xphb).

Treasure. Stashed in the junk pile are 25 GP and one magic item; roll once on the [Magic Items](#hotb,4,magic%20items,0) table in [appendix A](#hotb,4,appendix%20a%3a%20equipment%20and%20magic%20items,0) (rerolling duplicates) to determine the item.

Treasure. Treasure entries like this describe loot in an area. Usually, treasure consists of Gold Pieces (GP), magic items, or both.

Whenever a player character finds gold, give that player Gold Pieces equal to the listed value, which is tracked on their character sheet. For magic items, roll once on the [Magic Items](#hotb,4,magic%20items,0) table in [appendix A](#hotb,4,appendix%20a%3a%20equipment%20and%20magic%20items,0) (rerolling duplicates) to determine the item.

* Share the Wealth. Encourage players to split treasure among the characters as evenly as possible. This is especially important for magic items, which are seldom awarded.
* Spending Gold Pieces. Opportunities to spend gold in the Caves of Chaos are rare, but the [Keep on the Borderlands](#hotb,2,keep%20on%20the%20borderlands,0) contains plenty of goods and services available for purchase.

### A5: Larder

The stone door to this room is ajar. A persistent, ear-splitting cry emanates within. When the characters enter, read the following text aloud:

This chamber contains barrels and sacks of dried and salted meats, grains, and vegetables. A dragon wyrmling with copper scales sits on a scant pile of coins and eggshell fragments in the corner of the room, shrieking endlessly.

Two small, bipedal reptiles with worried expressions deposit handfuls of coins and junk at the creature's feet.

Two [Kobold Warriors](bestiary.html#kobold%20warrior_xmm) attempt to comfort the crying [Copper Dragon Wyrmling](bestiary.html#copper%20dragon%20wyrmling_xmm) by adding treasure to its meager hoard. It isn't going well. The kobolds and the wyrmling are [Indifferent](variantrules.html#indifferent%20%5battitude%5d_xphb) toward all other creatures.

Svetlin Velinov

Soothing the Wyrmling. The wyrmling knows the kobolds aren't its parents, but it doesn't know where its nest is. The wyrmling doesn't yet have a name, which only compounds its misery.

As an [Influence](actions.html#influence_xphb) action, a character can try to calm the wyrmling and make one of the following ability checks. Consider these examples:

**DC 10 Charisma (Deception).** The character mollifies the wyrmling with a promise they don't intend to keep.

**DC 10 Charisma (Persuasion).** The character assures the wyrmling they're here to help.

**DC 10 Charisma (Performance).** The character soothes the wyrmling with a gentle lullaby.

A character who offers the wyrmling at least 10 GP has Advantage on this check. On a successful check, the wyrmling stops wailing, and its attitude improves to [Friendly](variantrules.html#friendly%20%5battitude%5d_xphb), as do the attitudes of all kobolds in the cave. The wyrmling then asks the characters whether it should stay with the kobolds or return home. The kobolds honor the wyrmling's choice, which corresponds with the characters' recommendation.

Advantage/Disadvantage. Sometimes a [D20 Test](variantrules.html#d20%20test_xphb) is modified by special situations called [Advantage](variantrules.html#advantage_xphb) and [Disadvantage](variantrules.html#disadvantage_xphb), which reflect positive and negative circumstances, respectively. See the [D&D Beyond Basic Rules](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024) for more on Advantage and Disadvantage.

In area A5, if a character gives the dragon wyrmling at least 10 GP, that character has Advantage on an ability check to influence it.

* Roll Two D20s. When a roll has either Advantage or Disadvantage, roll a second d20 when you make the roll. Use the higher of the two rolls if you have Advantage, and use the lower roll if you have Disadvantage.
* Example. A Cleric gives the wyrmling 10 GP and then hums a tune to soothe it. The Cleric takes the [Influence](actions.html#influence_xphb) action and makes a Charisma (Performance) check with Advantage. The player rolls two d20s: 15 and 6. The player uses the higher roll and adds the Cleric's Performance modifier (+2) for a total of 17, which exceeds the DC of 10, and the check succeeds. If the check had been made with Disadvantage instead, the total of 8 would've failed.

On a failed check, or if the characters try to steal from its meager hoard, the wyrmling fights. The cowardly kobolds abstain from battle. If the wyrmling defeats all the characters, they wake up 1 hour later outside the cave with 1 Hit Point each.

Saving Throws. The wyrmling's Acid Breath action (see the wyrmling's stat block) prompts another type of [D20 Test](variantrules.html#d20%20test_xphb): a saving throw. Saving throws—also called saves—are named for the ability modifiers they use; in this case, characters use Dexterity to dodge an exhalation of acid. If a character is targeted by this effect, the player rolls a d20 and adds the character's Dexterity save modifier. A save's result is detailed in the effect that caused it.

Treasure. The wyrmling's hoard consists of 25 GP and one magic item; roll once on the [Magic Items](#hotb,4,magic%20items,0) table in [appendix A](#hotb,4,appendix%20a%3a%20equipment%20and%20magic%20items,0) (rerolling duplicates) to determine the item.
